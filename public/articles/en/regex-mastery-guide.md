<h4>The Power and Peril of Regex</h4><p>Regular expressions are the digital scalpel of developers, allowing you to extract, validate, and mutate complex data streams in milliseconds. Yet, poorly written regex can become a performance nightmare.</p><ul><li><strong>Anchors & Boundaries:</strong> Start your patterns with <code>^</code> and end with <code>$</code> to match entire strings. Use <code>\b</code> for word boundaries to avoid partial matches.</li><li><strong>Capture Groups vs. Non-Capturing:</strong> Use <code>(?:...)</code> if you don't need to extract the match later. It significantly improves regex engine performance.</li><li><strong>Lookarounds:</strong> Utilize positive <code>(?=...)</code> and negative <code>(?!...)</code> lookaheads for advanced assertions without consuming characters in the string.</li></ul><h4>Catastrophic Backtracking (ReDoS)</h4><p><strong>Warning:</strong> A poorly optimized regex engine can crash a Node.js single-threaded server. Patterns like <code>(a+)+$</code> applied to long strings will cause exponential backtracking. Always bound your quantifiers (e.g., <code>{1,50}</code>) instead of using <code>*</code> when accepting user input.</p><h4>ToolOrbit Regex Sandbox</h4><p>Debugging regex blindly is frustrating.
