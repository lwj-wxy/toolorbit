# Ecommerce Payment Processing Fees: Etsy, Stripe, PayPal, and Your Real Margin

Ecommerce sellers often compare fees in the wrong direction. Etsy fees, Stripe fees, and PayPal fees do not represent the same kind of cost. Etsy charges for marketplace access and order processing. Stripe and PayPal mainly process payments, though PayPal may also add wallet, checkout, invoice, international, and currency-related layers.

The useful question is not "Which fee is lowest?" The useful question is: "After fees, traffic cost, conversion, support, and refunds, which channel leaves the best real margin?"

## Marketplace Fees vs Payment Processor Fees

Etsy is a marketplace. Its fees help fund search visibility, buyer trust, marketplace infrastructure, and payment handling. A standalone website using Stripe or PayPal does not automatically bring buyers. You may pay less per transaction but spend more on traffic, content, ads, email, and support.

Here is the broad distinction:

| Channel | What the fee pays for |
|---------|-----------------------|
| Etsy | Marketplace traffic, listing infrastructure, transaction layer, payments, seller tools |
| Stripe | Card and payment processing infrastructure |
| PayPal | Wallet checkout, card processing, buyer trust, invoices, payment links |

That is why comparing Etsy's all-in fee to Stripe's payment fee can be misleading unless you also account for the cost of acquiring the customer.

## Start With Per-Order Math

For each channel, calculate:

```text
customer payment
- platform fees
- payment processing fees
- ads or acquisition cost
- item cost
- shipping and packaging cost
- refunds and support allowance
= real contribution margin
```

## Example: Etsy vs Standalone Website

Imagine a $50 product with $5 shipping charged to the buyer and $18 in direct cost.

On Etsy, you may face:

- listing fee
- transaction fee
- Etsy payment processing
- possible Offsite Ads
- possible regulatory or currency conversion fees

On your own website, you may face:

- Stripe or PayPal processing fees
- ecommerce platform subscription
- app/plugin costs
- payment dispute risk
- traffic acquisition cost
- email and retargeting tools

The independent website may have lower payment processing fees, but it is not automatically cheaper if every order requires paid traffic.

## When Independent Checkout Helps

Standalone payment processing can be attractive when:

- you already have repeat customers
- your email list drives sales without ad spend
- average order value is high
- customers buy bundles or subscriptions
- you need more checkout control
- marketplace fees are limiting margins
- you want to own customer data and post-purchase flows

In these cases, Stripe or PayPal fees may be easier to absorb than marketplace fees.

## When Etsy Still Makes Sense

Etsy can still be profitable when:

- search demand inside Etsy is strong
- buyers discover you through marketplace search
- you sell handmade, vintage, craft, or niche gift products
- your shop benefits from Etsy trust
- your conversion rate is meaningfully higher on Etsy than on your own site
- you have not yet built an independent audience

The marketplace fee is only expensive if it does not produce buyers.

## Why Stripe vs PayPal Belongs in the Same Workflow

Once you build an independent checkout, processor choice matters. Stripe may be cheaper for standard US domestic online card transactions, while PayPal can improve checkout trust for some buyers. The best configuration may be to offer both, then monitor real conversion and fee reports.

Use this workflow:

1. Estimate Etsy profit with the Etsy calculator.
2. Estimate Stripe payout for the same order amount.
3. Estimate PayPal payout for the same order amount.
4. Compare Stripe and PayPal side by side.
5. Add customer acquisition cost to the standalone website scenario.
6. Decide which channel deserves more promotion.

## Key Takeaways

1. Etsy fees and payment processor fees are different categories of cost.
2. Stripe and PayPal usually reduce the payment-processing layer, not the traffic-acquisition layer.
3. Net margin depends on order value, direct cost, conversion, refunds, ads, and repeat purchase behavior.
4. Sellers should compare channels with real scenarios, not headline percentages.
5. A healthy ecommerce strategy may use Etsy for discovery and a standalone checkout for repeat customers.
